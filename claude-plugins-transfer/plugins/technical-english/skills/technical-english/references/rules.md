# ASD-STE100 writing rules — quick reference

This is a summary of the writing rules of ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, in the order of
the sections of the standard. It is not the standard. For the full rules and the full dictionary,
get the current issue of ASD-STE100 from the ASD STE maintenance group (asd-ste100.org). The
specification is free of charge on request.

## Section 1 — Words

- Use approved words from the dictionary. Use them only in their approved meaning and as their
  approved part of speech.
- Use technical names: names of things in your domain (code identifiers, tools, parts, files,
  units, products). Keep their exact spelling.
- Use technical verbs only for actions specific to your domain (for software: "compile", "commit",
  "deploy", "merge", "run"). Do not use a technical verb when an approved verb has the same meaning.
- One word, one meaning. One meaning, one word. Do not change words for variety.
- Do not use slang, jargon that is not a technical name, or contractions ("don't" → "do not").
- Use American English spelling.

## Section 2 — Noun clusters

- Do not use more than three nouns in a cluster. If a technical name has more than three words,
  you can keep it, but explain it the first time.
- Break long clusters with prepositions, relative clauses or hyphens.

## Section 3 — Verbs

- Use only these forms: infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple future
  ("will" + verb), past participle as an adjective, and the past participle with "is/are" in
  descriptive text.
- Do not use `-ing` forms as verbs, except in technical names.
- Use the active voice in procedures. Use the active voice as much as possible in descriptive text.
- Use an approved verb for the action. Do not change a verb into a noun ("make an adjustment" →
  "adjust").
- Do not use phrasal verbs that are not approved.

## Section 4 — Sentences

- One topic in each sentence.
- Use connecting words ("then", "but", "if", "when", "because") to join sentences in a logical
  sequence.
- Do not omit words to make sentences shorter (keep articles "a", "an", "the", and "that").
- Use a vertical list for complex text.

## Section 5 — Procedures

- Sentence length: 20 words maximum.
- Write one instruction in each sentence. Two actions in one sentence are permitted only when you
  must do them at the same time.
- Use the imperative (command) form.
- Put a condition at the start of the sentence ("If…", "When…").
- Put a note (information, not an instruction) after the step it applies to.

## Section 6 — Descriptive writing

- Sentence length: 25 words maximum.
- Give the most important information first.
- One topic in each paragraph. Six sentences maximum in each paragraph.
- Use the key words of the topic to help the reader find information.

## Section 7 — Safety instructions (warnings and cautions)

- A warning tells about a risk of injury. A caution tells about a risk of damage to equipment or
  data.
- Start with a simple and clear command. Then give the risk or the result.
- Example: "Do not push to `main`. The gate does not run on direct pushes."

## Section 8 — Punctuation and word counts

- Use the colon, the semicolon and parentheses only when necessary.
- Count a number, a technical name or a unit as one word. Count a hyphenated word as one word.
- Use a full stop at the end of each sentence and each complete instruction.

## Section 9 — Writing practices

- Use the same words and the same structure for the same type of information.
- Write for the reader: give the information they need to do the task, not more.
- Show the result of an action when it is not clear.
